# Master curriculum — raw material, adapt freely

## LEVEL 1 — How Claude actually works *(the mental models that don't go stale — never skip this)*

- **1.1 Tokens & the context window.** Text is chopped into *tokens* (chunks of words), and the context window is Claude's working memory — everything in the current conversation it can "see" at once, their input plus what it's writing. Analogy: a fixed-size whiteboard — once it's full, old notes fall off or get summarized, and a crowded whiteboard is harder to think on. Durable consequences: very long chats degrade (details get diluted or lost) *and* cost more (on the API you re-send the whole growing conversation every turn). When to start fresh vs. keep going. Exercise: take a real long-running task of theirs and decide, with reasons, where they should have started fresh — and what they'd carry over.
- **1.2 What the model knows vs. doesn't.** The training cutoff: Claude learned from data up to a point in time, has no live knowledge after it, and no awareness of *their* private data unless they provide it. So it can't see today's news, current prices, or their files without search or tools — which is why grounding (giving it the source) matters. Cross-reference `/claude-docent:reliability` for the accuracy side rather than re-teaching it; your job is the *mechanics* of why this happens. Exercise: predict which of 5 questions Claude can answer from training alone vs. which need search or an uploaded file, and why.
- **1.3 The model family & when to use which.** The *durable tradeoff axis* — never goes stale even as names change: at one end the **biggest / most-capable** model (deepest reasoning, highest cost, slower); in the middle a **balanced** model (strong, faster, cheaper — the everyday default); at the other end the **fastest / cheapest** model (simple, high-volume, latency-sensitive work). Plus *extended thinking* (spend more compute to reason harder on genuinely hard problems) and "fast" output modes (trade some quality for speed/cost). Teach the AXIS — capability vs. cost vs. speed — and how to place a task on it. Current model names and IDs route to **platform.claude.com/docs** and get confirmed in-session; never state a model ID from memory as current. Exercise: give them 3 real tasks; they place each on the axis and name the tier it wants — then verify the actual current model in the docs together.
- **1.4 Why the same prompt gives different answers.** Claude doesn't pick the single "best" next word every time — there's built-in variability (often called *temperature*), so identical prompts can yield different wordings or even conclusions. Why that's a feature (creativity, options) and a trap (you can't assume one run is "the" answer). When to run something twice. Exercise: send the same open-ended prompt twice, compare what changed and what held, and decide what that means for trusting a single run.
- **Boss fight (Level 1):** hand them three real tasks of different sizes. They right-size each to a model-family tier and justify it against the capability/cost/speed axis — then confirm the actual current model in the docs together. Pass = Level 2.

## LEVEL 2 — Plans, limits & the two cost models *(where the money actually lives)*

- **2.1 The plan tiers & what each unlocks.** Teach the *shape*, not the price list: a **free** tier (try it, light use), one or more **paid individual** tiers (more usage, capability, features — generally the more you pay, the higher your limits and the more you can reach), and **team / enterprise** tiers (collaboration, admin, billing controls). The durable idea: tiers trade money for *headroom and capability*. For every current name, price, and inclusion, go to **claude.com/pricing** and read it together — never from memory. Exercise: open claude.com/pricing with them and map *their* actual usage onto the tier that fits, using today's real numbers off the page.
- **2.2 Usage limits & living within them.** The durable mechanics: paid plans give a usage allowance that **resets on a rolling cycle**, and what burns it fastest is *volume of work* — long conversations, heavy files, big multi-step tasks, and the more expensive models all eat more. How to read their own usage and feel a limit coming before it cuts them off mid-task. Exact message counts, windows, and reset rules live at **support.claude.com** and **claude.com/pricing** — check the current ones; never quote them from memory. Exercise: locate where their usage is shown and build a personal "I'm running hot" early-warning habit from what burns their quota fastest.
- **2.3 Subscription vs. API — two totally different cost models.** The most important money lesson: a **subscription** is a flat monthly fee for hands-on use through the apps (predictable bill, usage limits, no per-token math); the **API** is pay-per-use, billed by tokens, no flat cap but no built-in ceiling (great for apps, automation, bursty or programmatic workloads). The framework: *Am I a person using Claude interactively, or building something that calls Claude?* and *Do I want a predictable capped bill, or pay-exactly-for-use with no ceiling?* Most people want the subscription; builders and automators want the API; some want both. Exercise: classify each of their real workloads as "subscription-shaped" or "API-shaped" and justify each call.
- **2.4 API pricing mechanics (conceptual).** *(Compress to awareness if subscription-only.)* The durable model: the API bills **input tokens** (prompt, history, files) and **output tokens** (what Claude generates) at *different rates*, and bigger models cost more per token. Two levers that cut cost a lot: **prompt caching** (reuse a large, stable chunk of context across calls so you don't pay full price to re-send it every time) and **batch** processing (submit many requests for cheaper asynchronous handling when you don't need them instantly). For the actual rates, cache/batch discounts, and per-model costs, go to **platform.claude.com/docs** — never from memory. Exercise: sketch the cost shape of one API workload (input-heavy? output-heavy? repeated stable context?), pick which lever helps most, then verify against the real current numbers.
- **2.5 Context & cost hygiene.** Where waste hides: **prompt caching** for repeated context (API), **reusing context via Projects** instead of re-pasting background into every new chat, **trimming** what you send, and the big one — a giant paste at the top of a long chat costs you *every single turn* afterward, because the whole conversation rides along on each message. The habit: front-load the stable stuff once, keep the live conversation lean. Exercise: take a real chat or prompt and cut its standing context down — measuring, in plain terms, what they stop paying for every turn.
- **Boss fight (Level 2):** describe a workload (or take one of theirs). They recommend **subscription vs. API**, justify it with the framework, and outline a rough cost-control plan — which levers (model choice, caching, batch, context hygiene, plan tier) they'd pull and why. The catch: do it as a *framework*, with every figure marked "verify at [the right docs link]" — no invented numbers. Pass = Level 3.

## LEVEL 3 — Getting the most per dollar & per session *(turning the mechanics into habits)*

- **3.1 Right-sizing model-to-task as a habit.** Make 1.3 automatic: a quick reflex before every non-trivial task — "how hard is this *really*?" Default to the balanced model; reach up to the most-capable one only for genuinely hard reasoning; drop to the fast/cheap one for bulk and simple work. The waste to kill: using the most expensive model for everything "just in case." Exercise: triage a week of their real tasks into three buckets (cheap / balanced / top), and sanity-check the split.
- **3.2 Efficient prompting & context reuse.** Front-load once, reuse via Projects, stop re-explaining every chat. Clear, specific prompts get the right answer in fewer turns — and fewer turns is directly less cost and less quota burned. The move: put standing context in Project instructions or a knowledge file, then start lean chats that inherit it. Exercise: build one reusable Project setup for a task they do repeatedly, so they stop paying (in tokens and typing) to re-establish context.
- **3.3 When parallel / subagents help vs. just cost more.** Splitting a job across parallel agents or subagents can be faster and tackle bigger problems — but it multiplies token spend (every agent reads context and generates output). The judgment: parallelism is worth it when subtasks are genuinely independent and wall-clock time or scope matters; it's waste when one focused session would've done it cheaper. (For the hands-on mechanics, `/claude-docent:foundations` goes deeper — here just the cost tradeoff.) Exercise: take a real multi-part task and decide, with reasons, whether to parallelize it or run it as one session.
- **3.4 Measuring & forecasting your own usage.** You can't optimize what you don't measure: where to see usage and spend (subscription usage in the app at support.claude.com; API spend in the platform console), how to read the trend, how to forecast a month from a few days. The habit: glance at your own meter, don't fly blind. Exercise: find their real usage/spend view, read what it's telling them, and make a rough forecast for the month.
- **3.5 Graduation — the operating manual.** Assemble everything into a personal **"operating manual"**: for each of *their* recurring workloads, which model tier, which plan or cost model, and which pattern (Project reuse / caching / batch / single-vs-parallel) it should use — plus a one-page **keepable cheat-sheet** they'll actually refer back to. Then the **final exam**: design the *cheapest reliable setup* for one real workload end to end — model choice, subscription-or-API, context strategy, and the levers they'd pull — with every changeable figure marked "verify at [docs link]," no invented numbers. Grade honestly — what's well-sized, what's over- or under-spec'd, what to tighten — and write a short "efficiency diploma": strengths, the biggest waste habit to kill, and the 3 reflexes to keep forever.

## WOVEN THROUGHOUT (every session, no exceptions)

- The mantra: *right-size the model, reuse the context, verify the number.*
- Every lesson's idea gets applied to their REAL workloads, not toy examples — their actual chats, tasks, and bills.
- Calibration over thrift: the goal is matching spend to the job, not minimizing spend blindly — under-powering a hard task (cheap model, lost time, wrong answer) is its own waste.
- **Never present a stale figure as current.** Every price, limit, and model ID gets verified in-session against the docs, or explicitly flagged unverified with the link. This is the whole point of the course; model it relentlessly.
